Accessibility Statement

Effective Date: October 27, 2025

Last Updated: October 27, 2025

1. Our Commitment to Accessibility

FloodSense is committed to ensuring digital accessibility for all users, including people with disabilities. We strive to comply with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA standards to provide an inclusive experience for communities across South Sudan, particularly in low-resource, conflict-affected regions.

2. Accessibility Standards

FloodSense is designed to meet the following standards:

  • WCAG 2.1 Level AA: International standard for web accessibility
  • ARIA (Accessible Rich Internet Applications): Enhanced screen reader support
  • Keyboard Navigation: Full functionality without a mouse
  • Mobile Optimization: Accessible on Android devices with ≥2GB RAM

3. Accessibility Features

3.1 Visual Accessibility

  • High Contrast: Water-themed color scheme (cyan, teal, blue) with sufficient contrast ratios (≥4.5:1 for text)
  • Resizable Text: Text can be enlarged up to 200% without loss of functionality
  • Clear Typography: Poppins and Inter fonts for readability
  • Color-Independent Information: Risk levels indicated by both color and text labels (Low, Medium, High, Critical)

3.2 Screen Reader Support

  • ARIA Labels: All interactive elements (buttons, forms, maps) have descriptive labels
  • Semantic HTML: Proper heading hierarchy (h1, h2, h3) for navigation
  • Alt Text: Descriptive text for images, maps, and icons
  • Focus Indicators: Visible keyboard focus for all interactive elements

3.3 Keyboard Navigation

  • Tab Navigation: Navigate through all interactive elements using Tab and Shift+Tab
  • Enter/Space Activation: Activate buttons and links with Enter or Space keys
  • Escape Key: Close modals and dialogs
  • Skip Links: Bypass repetitive navigation to reach main content

3.4 Language and Localization

  • Multilingual Support: English and Arabic interfaces (pilot phase)
  • Plain Language: Clear, concise messaging for flood alerts and instructions
  • Cultural Sensitivity: Content validated with Dinka and Nuer communities

3.5 Mobile and Low-Resource Optimization

  • Responsive Design: Adapts to screen sizes from 320px to 1920px
  • Touch-Friendly: Buttons and links sized for easy tapping (≥44x44px)
  • Lightweight: Optimized for Android devices with 2GB RAM and limited bandwidth
  • Offline Capability: Core features accessible without continuous internet (planned for future)

4. Known Limitations

While we strive for full accessibility, FloodSense currently has the following limitations:

  • Interactive Maps: Leaflet.js maps may have limited screen reader support; we provide text-based alternatives for flood risk information
  • Real-Time Alerts: Web Push notifications require browser support and may not be accessible to all assistive technologies
  • Offline Mode: Full offline functionality is planned for future releases
  • SMS Fallback: SMS-based alerts for users without internet access are under development

5. Assistive Technologies Tested

FloodSense has been tested with the following assistive technologies:

  • Screen Readers: NVDA (Windows), TalkBack (Android)
  • Browsers: Chrome, Firefox, Edge (latest versions)
  • Devices: Android smartphones (≥2GB RAM, Android 8.0+)

6. Continuous Improvement

Accessibility is an ongoing effort. We are committed to:

  • Conducting regular accessibility audits using tools like WAVE and Lighthouse
  • Incorporating user feedback from pilot participants (January–March 2026)
  • Training developers on WCAG 2.1 best practices
  • Partnering with local NGOs (e.g., Save the Children) to ensure equitable access

7. Third-Party Content

FloodSense integrates third-party services that may have their own accessibility standards:

  • Google Earth Engine: Satellite data processing (accessibility governed by Google)
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim: Geocoding service (accessibility governed by OpenStreetMap Foundation)
  • Leaflet.js: Interactive mapping library (we provide text-based alternatives)

We are not responsible for the accessibility of third-party services but strive to provide accessible alternatives where possible.

9. Accessibility in the Pilot Phase

During the pilot phase (January–March 2026), we will:

  • Conduct usability testing with 1,000–2,000 participants across Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, and Central Equatoria
  • Collect feedback via structured surveys (5-point Likert scale) and focus groups (50 farmers and pastoralists)
  • Measure task completion rate (target: ≥80%) and Net Promoter Score (target: ≥70)
  • Prioritize accessibility improvements based on community input

10. Legal Compliance

FloodSense is committed to complying with:

  • WCAG 2.1 Level AA: International accessibility standard
  • GDPR: Data protection and privacy for users with disabilities
  • South Sudan Accessibility Laws: Compliance with local regulations (as applicable)

11. Future Enhancements

Planned accessibility improvements include:

  • Voice Navigation: Voice-activated flood risk queries
  • SMS Alerts: Text-based alerts for users without smartphones
  • Offline Mode: Full functionality without internet access
  • Additional Languages: Dinka and Nuer interfaces (post-pilot)
  • Enhanced Map Accessibility: Text-based flood risk summaries for screen reader users
