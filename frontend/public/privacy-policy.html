Privacy Policy

Effective Date: November 21, 2025

Last Updated: November 21, 2025

1. Introduction

FloodSense is a community-based predictive flood forecasting and early warning system developed as a BSc. Software Engineering project by John Akech under the supervision of Samiratu Ntohsi. This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use, protect, and share your personal information in compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and ethical principles outlined in our research framework.

2. Information We Collect

2.1 Personal Information

  • User Registration: Email, name, role (Community Member, NGO Partner, Admin), language preference (English, Arabic)
  • Location Data: Geographic coordinates (latitude, longitude) for flood predictions and alerts
  • Feedback Data: Community reports on flood events, system usability, and alert effectiveness

2.2 Technical Information

  • Device Information: Device type, operating system, browser type
  • Usage Data: Pages visited, features used, time spent on platform
  • API Logs: Request timestamps, endpoints accessed, response times

2.3 Satellite and Environmental Data

  • Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, CHIRPS rainfall data, SRTM elevation data, WorldPop population estimates (processed via Google Earth Engine)

3. How We Use Your Information

3.1 Primary Purposes

  • Flood Prediction: Generate flood probability forecasts using Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and Temporal Convolutional Network models
  • Early Warning Delivery: Send real-time flood alerts via Web Push notifications
  • Infrastructure Recommendations: Provide GIS-based dyke placement guidance
  • System Improvement: Analyze feedback to refine models and enhance usability

3.2 Research and Validation

  • Validate predictions against community-reported flood events
  • Conduct pilot evaluation (January–March 2026) across Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, and Central Equatoria
  • Assess displacement reduction (target: ≥20%) and system performance (Current F1-score: 97.78%, Latency: < 500ms)

4. Data Protection and Security

4.1 Encryption

  • AES-256 Encryption: All user data and predictions are encrypted at rest and in transit
  • Secure Storage: Encryption keys stored in protected environment variables

4.2 Authentication and Access Control

  • JWT Authentication: Secure token-based access with 24-hour expiration
  • Role-Based Access: Permissions limited by user role (Community Member, NGO Partner, Admin)

4.3 API Security

  • CORS Policies: Restrict requests to authorized domains
  • Rate Limiting: Maximum 100 requests per hour to prevent abuse
  • Input Sanitization: DOMPurify prevents XSS attacks; CSRF tokens protect state-changing requests

4.4 Compliance

  • Adheres to GDPR principles and OWASP Top 10 security standards

5. Data Sharing and Disclosure

5.1 With Your Consent

  • Share anonymized flood event data with NGO partners (e.g., Save the Children, World Food Programme) for humanitarian response

5.2 Legal Requirements

  • Disclose data if required by law or to protect public safety during emergencies

5.3 Research Purposes

  • Anonymized, aggregated data may be used for academic publications and system scalability studies (e.g., adaptation to Nigeria, Tanzania)

5.4 No Third-Party Sales

  • We do not sell or rent your personal information to third parties

6. Your Rights

6.1 Access and Correction

  • Request access to your personal data and correct inaccuracies via the web app or by contacting j.akech@alustudent.com

6.2 Data Deletion

  • Request deletion of your account and associated data (subject to legal retention requirements)

6.3 Withdrawal of Consent

  • Opt out of data collection or alert notifications at any time

6.4 Data Portability

  • Request a copy of your data in a machine-readable format (e.g., JSON, CSV)

7. Data Retention

  • User Accounts: Retained for the duration of system use plus 2 years for research validation
  • Flood Predictions: Stored for 5 years to support model retraining and historical analysis
  • Feedback Data: Retained for 3 years for system improvement
  • Logs: API logs retained for 90 days for security monitoring

8. Cookies and Tracking

FloodSense uses minimal cookies for:

  • Authentication: JWT tokens stored in secure, HTTP-only cookies
  • Preferences: Language selection (English, Arabic)
  • Analytics: Anonymous usage statistics to improve system performance

You can disable cookies in your browser settings, but this may limit functionality.

9. Children's Privacy

FloodSense is designed for users aged 13 and above. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided personal information, contact us immediately at j.akech@alustudent.com.

10. International Data Transfers

Data is processed in South Sudan and may be transferred to cloud servers (e.g., AWS Lambda) for hosting. All transfers comply with GDPR safeguards, including encryption and contractual protections.

11. Changes to This Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy to reflect system enhancements or legal requirements. Changes will be posted on this page with an updated "Last Updated" date. Continued use of FloodSense constitutes acceptance of the revised policy.

12. Ethical Considerations and Frameworks

FloodSense is committed to the "do no harm" principle, particularly in South Sudan's conflict-affected context. The project adheres to multiple ethical frameworks and has obtained ethical clearance from the ALU Research Ethics Committee.

12.1 Ethical Standards

  • ALU Research Ethics Framework: Guides informed consent, privacy protection, and responsible data sharing
  • South Sudan National Research Ethics Guidelines: Ensures culturally sensitive interactions with diverse communities
  • ACM Code of Ethics (2018): Promotes transparency and fairness in system design
  • UN-Habitat's Right to the City (2010): Supports inclusion and equality for vulnerable communities

12.2 Respect for Vulnerable Groups

  • Special care for elders, displaced persons, and minors in flood-prone communities
  • Informed consent obtained in Dinka, Nuer, and Arabic languages
  • Voluntary participation without pressure, respecting local traditions
  • Cultural sensitivity guides all interactions with communities

12.3 Enhanced Data Protection

  • AES-256 encryption for all sensitive data including user geolocations and survey feedback
  • Anonymization to remove personal identifiers before analysis
  • Access restricted to authorized research personnel only
  • Secure SQLite databases with role-based access controls
  • Data retained only as long as necessary for research purposes (see Section 7)

12.4 AI Fairness and Bias Mitigation

  • Regular audits of AI models for bias across ethnic groups (Dinka, Nuer)
  • Fairness metrics: equal opportunity difference ≤0.05
  • Community validation to reduce wetland mapping bias
  • Avoiding harmful labeling or misrepresentation of individuals or groups
  • Data analyzed to highlight trends, not to blame specific communities

12.5 Community Oversight and Engagement

  • Partnership with local NGOs (Save the Children, World Food Programme) for oversight
  • Cultural sensitivity and equitable access monitoring
  • Aggregated data sharing to protect individual identities
  • Constructive reporting of infrastructure gaps without causing harm
  • Community feedback integrated into system improvements

12.6 Transparency and No Deception

  • Full transparency about research purpose and data use
  • Clear privacy notices in accessible language (English, Arabic)
  • No misleading statements or hidden objectives
  • Trust maintained throughout the research process
  • Participants receive full information before consent

12.7 Handling Sensitive Topics

  • Displacement and food insecurity approached with care and sensitivity
  • Focus on supporting meaningful solutions, not exploitation
  • Respect for experiences of flood-affected communities
  • Social and economic impact: enhancing opportunities, reducing displacement
  • No risk of job loss or social harm from system implementation

12.8 Responsible Use of Open-Source Tools

  • Proper credit given to developers of Google Earth Engine, FastAPI, ee-fastapi, and UN-SPIDER framework
  • Compliance with open-source licenses and terms of service
  • Ethical use of satellite data from public repositories (ESA, NASA)
